<section className="pr-sec">
<div className="pr-tag">Why this exists <b>01</b></div>
<p className="pr-body">Consequential decisions — in policy, in AI, in markets — increasingly turn on reasoning
that is opaque: the data behind it, the assumptions inside it, the people it affects. When the reasoning can't
be seen, it can't be challenged on substance, and disagreement collapses into who you trust. As those same
decisions move onto agent-to-agent rails, the problem compounds: a machine has to decide, in milliseconds,
whether to rely on a claim it cannot inspect.</p>
<p className="pr-body" style={{ marginTop: '14px' }}>OpenReason makes the reasoning an <strong>object</strong>:
structured, forkable, and — since v0.3 — cryptographically verifiable, so both a person and a machine can judge
<em>how far</em> to trust it.</p>
</section>

<section className="pr-sec">
<div className="pr-tag">The philosophy · REE <b>02</b></div>
<h2>Rational, Empathy-Informed Ethics</h2>
<p className="pr-body" style={{ marginBottom: '20px' }}>Five commitments about <em>method</em>, not conclusions.</p>
<div className="pr-grid">
  <div className="pr-cell"><div className="n">01</div><div className="t">Measured compassion</div><div className="d">Claims of benefit or harm must be testable with evidence, not asserted.</div></div>
  <div className="pr-cell"><div className="n">02</div><div className="t">Rational inquiry</div><div className="d">Assumptions are hypotheses to be stated and stress-tested, not settled facts.</div></div>
  <div className="pr-cell"><div className="n">03</div><div className="t">Simulated consequences</div><div className="d">Model outcomes before committing, and make the model re-runnable by others.</div></div>
  <div className="pr-cell"><div className="n">04</div><div className="t">Universal sentience</div><div className="d">No affected party is invisible; minorities are tested, not averaged away.</div></div>
  <div className="pr-cell"><div className="n">05</div><div className="t">Transparent accountability</div><div className="d">Every decision is logged, attributable, and auditable.</div></div>
</div>
</section>

<section className="pr-sec">
<div className="pr-tag">What constrains us <b>03</b></div>
<div className="pr-two">
  <div>
    <div className="pr-k">Neutral</div>
    <h3>Attest, never grade</h3>
    <p>ORP attests to <em>provenance and reasoning</em> — who said what, when, citing which sources and
    assumptions. It never issues a verdict on whether a claim is good or correct. The moment a standard grades
    content, it loses the neutrality that makes an attestation worth anything.</p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <div className="pr-k">Verifiable</div>
    <h3>Check without trusting</h3>
    <p>Every claim should be checkable without trusting the author — by hand for a person, by signature for a
    machine.</p>
  </div>
</div>
</section>

<section className="pr-sec" style={{ borderBottom: 'none' }}>
<div className="pr-tag">Origin — open, and built to outlast its founders <b>04</b></div>
<p className="pr-body">OpenReason was founded by the <a href="https://publicreasonproject.org">Public Reason
Project</a>, an open-standards project for epistemic accountability. Its argument — that every consequential
decision rests on data, and almost no one examines how that data was made — is why this protocol exists.
OpenReason is the project's first standard; the manifesto and foundations behind it live there.</p>
<p className="pr-body" style={{ marginTop: '14px' }}>OpenReason is an open protocol, not a product — anyone can implement it; no single entity
owns it. The code and specification are <strong>Apache-2.0</strong> (an explicit patent grant for a standard
others implement). The governance model is designed so the project can function without its founders:
disagreement is resolved by <strong>forking and showing better reasoning</strong>, not by vote.</p>
