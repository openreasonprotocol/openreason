# The Public Reason Project

The Public Reason Project builds open infrastructure for **warranted trust** — reliance grounded in
evidence you can check, not in authority or reputation. Its first standard is the
[OpenReason Protocol](/what-it-is). Everything we build is open, neutral, and verifiable.

## Why this exists

Consequential decisions — in policy, in AI, in markets — increasingly turn on reasoning that is opaque: the
data behind it, the assumptions inside it, the people it affects. When the reasoning can't be seen, it can't
be challenged on substance, and disagreement collapses into who you trust. As those same decisions move onto
agent-to-agent rails, the problem compounds: a machine has to decide, in milliseconds, whether to rely on a
claim it cannot inspect.

OpenReason makes the reasoning an **object**: structured, forkable, and — since v0.3 — cryptographically
verifiable, so both a person and a machine can judge *how far* to trust it.

## The philosophy — Rational, Empathy-Informed Ethics (REE)

OpenReason is grounded in five principles. They are commitments about method, not conclusions.

1. **Measured compassion** — claims of benefit or harm must be testable with evidence, not asserted.
2. **Rational inquiry** — assumptions are hypotheses to be stated and stress-tested, not settled facts.
3. **Simulated consequences** — model outcomes before committing, and make the model re-runnable by others.
4. **Universal sentience** — no affected party is invisible; minorities are tested, not averaged away.
5. **Transparent accountability** — every decision is logged, attributable, and auditable.

The deeper treatment lives in the project's academic work and the `REE_PHILOSOPHY` document in the repository.

## Neutral and verifiable

Two commitments constrain everything:

- **Neutral.** ORP attests to *provenance and reasoning* — who said what, when, citing which sources and
  assumptions. It never issues a verdict on whether a claim is good or correct. The moment a standard grades
  content, it loses the neutrality that makes an attestation worth anything.
- **Verifiable.** Every claim should be checkable without trusting the author — by hand for a person, by
  signature for a machine.

## Open & governed to outlast its founders

OpenReason is an open protocol, not a product — anyone can implement it; no single entity owns it. The code
and specification are **Apache-2.0** (an explicit patent grant for a standard others implement). The
governance model is designed so the project can function without its founders: disagreement is resolved by
**forking and showing better reasoning**, not by vote.
