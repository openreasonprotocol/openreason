# How it works

## 1. A document, in five layers

An ORP document is structured so that each part of the reasoning is separately inspectable and auditable:

| | Layer | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Data provenance | Where did the data come from — and what was excluded or cleaned? |
| **L2** | Consequence simulation | What outcomes were modelled, under which assumptions? |
| **L3** | Empathy mapping | Who is affected, and were minorities stress-tested? |
| **L4** | Accountability | Who decided what, when, and why? |
| **L5** | Lineage | What is this derived from, and what forks exist? |

## 2. Fork and re-run

Because assumptions are explicit, anyone can **fork** a document, change one assumption, and **re-run** it.
Disagreement becomes *locatable* — you can point to the exact assumption or source where two parties diverge,
instead of talking past each other.

## 3. Verify (v0.3)

A document can carry a cryptographic **attestation** — a detached Ed25519 signature over its canonical form.
A reader (a person, or an agent on a rail) verifies it **offline**: recompute the hash, check the signature
against a key it resolves itself, confirm the key hasn't expired. No need to trust, or even contact, the
publisher.

Verification yields a **trust tier** — `self_declared → attested → verified → full` — and a claimed tier
never exceeds what the signatures prove. For data products, a **usage policy** then says what may be done,
which a rail enforces at activation.
