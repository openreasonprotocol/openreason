## 1. GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)

### Official Source
**Full Title:** Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council
**URL:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj
**Adopted:** April 2016 | **Effective:** May 2018
**Type:** EU Regulation (legally binding)

### Key Articles Relevant to ORP

**Article 5 - Principles relating to processing**
- Lawfulness, fairness, transparency
- Purpose limitation
- Data minimization
- Accuracy
- Storage limitation
- **Relevance to ORP:** Principles but no provenance requirement

**Article 13-14 - Information to be provided**
- Identity of controller
- Purposes of processing
- Legal basis
- **Relevance to ORP:** Focuses on data subject rights, not data constitution

**Article 25 - Data protection by design and by default**
- **Relevance to ORP:** Closest to constitutive thinking, but implementation-focused

**Article 30 - Records of processing activities**
- Controller must maintain records: purposes, categories, recipients, transfers
- **Relevance to ORP:** This is what ORP L1 extends - records of processing vs. records of constitution

### What GDPR Does NOT Require
- Funding source disclosure
- Exclusion criteria documentation
- Scope decisions rationale
- Data constitution decisions (only processing decisions)

### Reading Notes
- GDPR is about **access rights** and **processing** (downstream of data creation)
- Strong on consent and purpose limitation
- Silent on how data assets are constituted
- Recital 26: Anonymous data outside scope (ORP addresses this too)

## 2. EU AI Act (Artificial Intelligence Act)

### Official Source
**Full Title:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 on Artificial Intelligence
**URL:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689
**Adopted:** June 2024 | **Effective:** Phased (2025-2027)
**Type:** EU Regulation (legally binding)

### Key Articles Relevant to ORP

**Article 10 - Data and data governance**
- Training, validation, testing datasets must be:
  - Relevant, sufficiently representative, error-free
  - Appropriate statistical properties
  - Free of biases
- **Relevance to ORP:** Closest to constitutive concern, but focuses on quality not provenance

**Article 11 - Technical documentation**
- Description of training data
- Data provenance
- **Relevance to ORP:** Requires documentation but not transparent decision-making about constitution

**Article 13 - Transparency obligations**
- Designed to be sufficiently transparent
- **Relevance to ORP:** Transparency of operation, not constitution

**Annex IV - Technical Documentation**
- Description of training methodologies
- Training data sources
- **Relevance to ORP:** This is where ORP L1 would extend AI Act requirements

### What AI Act Does NOT Require
- Funding relationships for training data
- Documentation of excluded populations
- Reasoning for scope decisions
- Alternatives considered in dataset construction
- Accountability ledger for training decisions

### Reading Notes
- AI Act addresses **model behavior** and **high-risk applications**
- Strong on risk categorization (prohibited/high-risk/limited-risk/minimal-risk)
- Data requirements are quality-focused, not constitution-focused
- No Layer 4 (decision accountability) equivalent

## 3. W3C PROV (Provenance Ontology)

### Official Source
**Full Title:** PROV-O: The PROV Ontology
**URL:** https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/
**Published:** 2013-04-30
**Type:** W3C Recommendation (technical standard)

### Core Concepts

**Three Main Classes:**
1. **prov:Entity** - Physical, digital, conceptual things
2. **prov:Activity** - How entities come into existence
3. **prov:Agent** - Responsible for activities

**Key Properties:**
- `prov:wasGeneratedBy` - Entity generated by activity
- `prov:wasAttributedTo` - Entity attributed to agent
- `prov:used` - Activity used entity
- `prov:wasAssociatedWith` - Activity associated with agent

### Relevance to ORP Layer 1

**What PROV covers:**
- Activity lineage (what process generated this entity)
- Attribution (who created it)
- Derivation chains (entity X derived from entity Y)

**What PROV does NOT cover:**
- **Funding sources** (agent attribution but not funding relationships)
- **Exclusion criteria** (what was deliberately not included)
- **Scope decisions** (why this activity boundary, not another)
- **Incentive structures** (agent motivations)
- **Alternatives considered** (decision reasoning)

### Reading Notes
- PROV is **structural** (graph of entities/activities/agents)
- ORP Layer 1 extends PROV with **constitutive decisions**
- PROV + ORP = "what happened" + "why it happened that way"
- Integration: ORP documents could reference PROV graphs

**Example of gap:**
```turtle
# PROV can express:
:dataset1 prov:wasGeneratedBy :data_collection_activity .
:data_collection_activity prov:wasAssociatedWith :researcher .

# PROV cannot express:
- Why this population was sampled (scope decision)
- Who funded the activity (funding relationship)
- What criteria excluded certain observations (exclusion)
- What alternatives were considered (decision reasoning)
```

## 4. ISO 19115 (Geographic Information - Metadata)

### Official Source
**Full Title:** ISO 19115-1:2014 Geographic information — Metadata
**URL:** https://www.iso.org/standard/53798.html (paywall)
**Published:** 2014 (updated from 2003 version)
**Type:** ISO Standard (international metadata standard)

### Overview
ISO 19115 defines metadata schema for geographic datasets:
- Identification (title, abstract, keywords)
- Constraints (legal, security)
- Data quality
- Spatial/temporal extent
- Distribution

### Relevant Sections

**Metadata Section:**
- Contact information
- Date stamps
- Metadata standard

**Identification Information:**
- Citation (title, date, responsible party)
- Abstract and purpose
- Status and update frequency

**Data Quality Information:**
- Lineage (sources, processing)
- Positional accuracy
- Completeness

**Lineage (closest to ORP L1):**
- Data sources
- Process steps
- **Gap:** Not funding, not exclusions, not decision reasoning

### What ISO 19115 Does NOT Cover
- Funding relationships
- Why exclusions were made (only what was excluded)
- Decision accountability (who decided what, when, why)
- Alternatives considered
- Constitutive incentive structures

### Reading Notes
- ISO 19115 is **descriptive metadata** (what the data is)
- ORP Layer 1 is **constitutive metadata** (how/why the data came to be)
- Lineage section is closest analog but purely technical (not decision-focused)
- Integration: ORP L1 could reference ISO 19115 records as sources

## 5. Model Cards for Model Reporting (Mitchell et al., 2019)

### Official Source
**Full Title:** Model Cards for Model Reporting
**Authors:** Margaret Mitchell, Simone Wu, Andrew Zaldivar, Parker Barnes, Lucy Vasserman, Ben Hutchinson, Elena Spitzer, Inioluwa Deborah Raji, Timnit Gebru
**Published:** FAT* 2019 (ACM Conference on Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency)
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3287560.3287596
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993

### Model Card Structure

**Sections:**
1. **Model Details** - Who created, version, type, training algorithms
2. **Intended Use** - Primary use cases, out-of-scope uses
3. **Factors** - Relevant factors (demographic, environmental)
4. **Metrics** - Performance measures
5. **Evaluation Data** - Datasets used, preprocessing
6. **Training Data** - Datasets, preprocessing, motivation
7. **Quantitative Analyses** - Performance across factors
8. **Ethical Considerations** - Sensitive use cases
9. **Caveats and Recommendations** - Limitations

### Relevance to ORP

**What Model Cards Cover:**
- Model provenance (who, when, how trained)
- Dataset description (training + evaluation)
- Performance metrics
- Limitations and caveats

**What Model Cards Do NOT Cover (ORP Fills):**
- **Training data constitution decisions** (Model Cards describe data, ORP documents how it was constituted)
- **Funding relationships** for training data
- **Exclusion criteria** in data collection
- **Stakeholder impact** (Model Cards mention ethical considerations, ORP requires systematic empathy mapping)
- **Decision accountability** (Model Cards document model, ORP documents decisions)
- **Forks/alternatives** (Model Cards are static, ORP has fork registry)

### Reading Notes
- Model Cards = **model-level** transparency
- ORP = **data-level + decision-level** transparency
- Complementary: Model Card could reference ORP document for training data
- Gap: Model Cards focus on model behavior given data, not data constitution

**Integration example:**
```yaml
# ORP document for ImageNet training data (L1-L5)
# Model Card for ResNet-50 trained on ImageNet
# Model Card Section "Training Data" references ORP document
```

## 6. Datasheets for Datasets (Gebru et al., 2018)

### Official Source
**Full Title:** Datasheets for Datasets
**Authors:** Timnit Gebru, Jamie Morgenstern, Briana Vecchione, Jennifer Wortman Vaughan, Hanna Wallach, Hal Daumé III, Kate Crawford
**Published:** Communications of the ACM, March 2021 (originally arXiv 2018)
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3458723
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09010

### Datasheet Structure (7 Sections)

1. **Motivation**
   - Why was the dataset created?
   - Who funded creation?
   - **ORP relevance:** Closest to L1 funding disclosure

2. **Composition**
   - What data does it contain?
   - Is it a sample? If so, what population?
   - Any missing information?
   - **ORP relevance:** Partially overlaps L1, but not decision-focused

3. **Collection Process**
   - How was data collected?
   - Who was involved?
   - Over what timeframe?
   - **ORP relevance:** Overlaps L1 methodology

4. **Preprocessing/Cleaning/Labeling**
   - Was preprocessing done?
   - Is raw data available?
   - **ORP relevance:** Overlaps L1 cleaning decisions

5. **Uses**
   - What tasks has dataset been used for?
   - Are there tasks it should NOT be used for?
   - **ORP relevance:** Partially overlaps L2 scenarios

6. **Distribution**
   - How distributed? Any restrictions?
   - **ORP relevance:** Overlaps L1 access

7. **Maintenance**
   - Who maintains dataset?
   - How will it evolve?
   - **ORP relevance:** Future versioning (L5 adjacent)

### What Datasheets Cover That ORP Also Covers
- Funding (Motivation section)
- Methodology (Collection section)
- Preprocessing decisions (Section 4)
- Limitations (distributed across sections)

### What ORP Adds Beyond Datasheets

| Datasheet Gap | ORP Layer | How ORP Addresses |
|---------------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Decision reasoning** | L1 + L4 | Why exclusions made, alternatives considered |
| **Incentive structures** | L1 | Explicit funding relationships + conflicts |
| **Consequence modeling** | L2 | Forward-looking scenario analysis |
| **Stakeholder impact** | L3 | Systematic empathy mapping (not just "uses") |
| **Accountability** | L4 | Attributed decision log with timestamps |
| **Contestability** | L5 | Fork registry for alternatives |

### Reading Notes
- Datasheets = **documentation** (descriptive)
- ORP = **accountability** (decision-focused + contestable)
- Datasheets answer "what was done"
- ORP answers "what was done + why + by whom + what else was considered"
- Complementary: Datasheet could be L1 component of full ORP document

## 7. FAIR Principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable)

### Official Source
**Full Title:** The FAIR Guiding Principles for scientific data management and stewardship
**Authors:** Mark D. Wilkinson et al.
**Published:** Scientific Data, March 2016
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2016.18
**URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/sdata201618

### The Four Principles

**F - Findable**
- (Meta)data assigned globally unique identifier
- Data described with rich metadata
- Indexed in searchable resource

**A - Accessible**
- Retrievable by identifier using standard protocol
- Metadata accessible even if data isn't
- Authentication/authorization where needed

**I - Interoperable**
- (Meta)data use formal, shared language
- Use vocabularies that follow FAIR
- Include qualified references to other data

**R - Reusable**
- Rich attributes with plural usage
- Clear usage license
- Provenance information
- Meet domain standards

### Relevance to ORP

**What FAIR Covers:**
- **Findability** - Dataset can be discovered
- **Accessibility** - Dataset can be retrieved
- **Interoperability** - Dataset can be integrated with others
- **Reusability** - Dataset can be used in new contexts

**What FAIR Does NOT Cover:**
- **Constitution** - How the dataset was created
- **Funding** - Who paid for data production
- **Exclusions** - What was deliberately omitted
- **Decisions** - Why scope/methods chosen
- **Stakeholders** - Who is affected
- **Alternatives** - What other approaches were considered

### Key Insight
FAIR is about **data as product** (how to make it usable)
ORP is about **data as process** (how it was constituted)

**FAIR ensures data is reusable. ORP ensures you know what you're reusing and under what conditions it was produced.**

### Reading Notes
- FAIR + ORP = Complete picture (reusability + constitution)
- FAIR R4 (provenance information) is entry point for ORP L1
- FAIR doesn't require *quality* of provenance, just presence
- Integration: FAIR-compliant dataset + ORP document = fully transparent

## 8. Open Data Charter

### Official Source
**Full Title:** International Open Data Charter
**URL:** https://opendatacharter.net/principles/
**Adopted:** 2015 (updated 2018)
**Type:** International principles (non-binding)

### Six Principles

1. **Open by Default**
   - Government data should be open unless legitimate reason not to be

2. **Timely and Comprehensive**
   - Published in a timely manner
   - Described comprehensively

3. **Accessible and Usable**
   - Available in open formats
   - Machine-readable

4. **Comparable and Interoperable**
   - Use standards
   - Enable comparison across sectors/borders

5. **For Improved Governance & Citizen Engagement**
   - Increase transparency
   - Enable participation

6. **For Inclusive Development and Innovation**
   - Support inclusive development

### Relevance to ORP

**What Open Data Charter Covers:**
- **Access** - Data should be public
- **Format** - Machine-readable, open standards
- **Timeliness** - Up-to-date publication
- **Purpose** - Transparency and participation goals

**What Open Data Charter Does NOT Cover:**
- **Provenance** - How data was collected/constituted
- **Funding** - Who paid for data production
- **Exclusions** - What populations/data omitted
- **Quality** - Accuracy, completeness, representativeness
- **Decision-making** - Accountability for data production choices
- **Stakeholder impact** - Effects on affected parties

### Key Insight
**Open Data Charter democratizes ACCESS to data**
**ORP democratizes UNDERSTANDING of data's constitution**

Open access to a distorted dataset does not correct the distortion.

### Reading Notes
- Open Data Charter = political/access principles
- ORP = epistemic/accountability principles
- Complementary: Open Data Charter gets data published, ORP makes its constitution transparent
- Gap: No requirement for provenance, quality documentation, or decision transparency

## Summary: Coverage Gaps

### What ALL Eight Standards Miss

**Constitutive-layer concerns that NO existing standard adequately addresses:**

1. **Funding influence documentation**
   - Who paid for data production?
   - What were their interests?
   - How might funding shape scope/methods?

2. **Exclusion reasoning**
   - What was excluded from dataset/study?
   - Why those exclusions (methodological vs. incentive-driven)?
   - Who does this exclusion affect?

3. **Decision accountability (Layer 4)**
   - Who made key methodology decisions?
   - When were they made?
   - What alternatives were considered?
   - Traceable decision log

4. **Forward-looking consequence modeling (Layer 2)**
   - What outcomes under different scenarios?
   - How do constitutive choices affect conclusions?
   - Sensitivity analysis to assumptions

5. **Systematic stakeholder impact (Layer 3)**
   - Who is affected by data/decisions?
   - Including absent stakeholders (not in data)
   - Impact analysis with minority stress-testing

6. **Contestability infrastructure (Layer 5)**
   - Formal mechanism for alternative assumptions
   - Fork registry tracking divergent approaches
   - Comparison of outcomes under different starting points
